Our Children

Two Bowls

for every parent who loves a child they cannot reach

You know the moment. I know you do. You walk in from the evening the way a tired person will, you take a seat, you lift the menu, and the room is calm and still.

Then a word rises up to meet you from a plain and printed line. For me it was a bowl of ramen. For you it’s some other sign: a cereal, a song, a sweater, a name called out across a store, a laugh that sounds like someone’s laugh who doesn’t laugh with you anymore.

And we sit there, don’t we? We sit there with the page, strangers eating all around us, and we feel it, every age they ever were come back to us like steam above the bowl. We don’t call the waiter. We let it fill the whole.

For grief is only love, my friend, with nowhere left to go, so it gathers in a mother, in a father, soft and slow.

We are many. Count us. We are at the counter and the light. We are the ones who set the table and still save a chair at night. We did not stop loving. We are loving still. We let go of the rope so they would not be torn. We did not let go of the will.

A child was never made to be a rope pulled hard from either end. Some of us have let go of our end, not to lose them, but so they would not bend and break the way the young can break when they are pulled too far. We let go of the rope, our loves. We did not let go of who you are.

So hear us now, you children, wherever you are tonight. The soup will cool, the night will close, the city keeps its light, but love is not a dish that cools, and love is never gone. It waits for you the way the morning waits for you to come along.

There is a seat beside us. There will always be a seat. Come next year, or come in twenty. Come hungry. Come complete. Come angry if you have to. Come with questions, come with doubt. We will order two bowls of anything, and we will work it out.

Still, we are here. Still, we are here. Still. Here
