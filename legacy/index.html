Each life overlaps the next, and each studied at the University of Virginia : 1834, 1890, 1925, 1987. From 1911 to 1932 the Law School stood in Minor Hall, named for John B. Minor.

Division I · The Teacher

John Barbee Minor

2 June 1813 – 29 July 1895

At sixteen he crossed Virginia on horseback as a newspaper agent, then walked to Ohio to enroll at Kenyon College. Dissatisfied, he walked back.

1834
Bachelor of Laws, University of Virginia, at twenty-one.
1845 – 1895
Professor of Common and Statute Law. Fifty years in one seat.
1853
His law classes move into the new Rotunda Annex.
1865
Walks out under a flag of truce to ask that the University be spared.
From 1875
The Institutes, four volumes, printed at his own expense.
1911
Minor Hall opens, named for him: the Law School’s home until 1932.

Division II · The Architect

Raleigh Colston Minor

1869 – 1923 · Professor of Constitutional and International Law

Three degrees from the University by twenty-one. When the Rotunda burned in 1895, he saved nearly all of the law books.

1887 – 1890
B.A., M.S. and LL.B., University of Virginia.
1893
His father’s assistant; on the law faculty until 1923.
1895
Saves nearly all of the law collection from the Rotunda fire.
1907 – 08
Acting dean of the Law School, University of Virginia.
1908
The Law of Real Property, two volumes, built on his father’s Institutes.
1918
A Republic of Nations, a year before the Covenant.

Division III · The Practitioner

Charles Venable Minor

1900 – 1988 · Charlottesville, Virginia · Known to his family as Hippo

The first two divisions taught and wrote. The third practiced, and practice does not leave a title page.

1922 · 1925
Bachelor of Science, University of Virginia; Law School, class of 1925, in Minor Hall, named for his grandfather.
1962 – 63
President of the Virginia State Bar.
The firm
Name partner in Perkins, Battle & Minor, then Battle, Neal, Harris, Minor & Williams. In 1966 the Charlottesville firm merged with Richmond’s McGuire, Woods, King, Davis & Patterson to form McGuire, Woods & Battle, today McGuireWoods.

Division III · The Ode

The hippo and the bird

When Halsey Minor started Minor Ventures, he made its logo a hippo with a small bird on its back. Its companies included GrandCentral, now Google Voice, and OpenDNS.

Division IV · The Reformation

Halsey Minor

1964 –

He did not go to law school. That is the departure. Everything after it is the return.

1987
Anthropology, University of Virginia.
Not changing
The ideal. Equal justice, and the conviction that it is owed rather than sold.
Changing
The venue and the incentive.
The claim
Not a reinvention of the law. A reformation of how ordinary people are required to meet it.

Division I · 3 March 1865

The flag of truce

Sheridan and Custer enter Charlottesville

He was too unwell to attend the meeting at which he was appointed. He went out anyway.

Between one and two o’clock he went with Mayor Christopher Fowler, faculty chairman Socrates Maupin, and Rector Thomas Preston to the ground opposite Carr’s Hill, where a flag of truce had already been raised.

Custer passed in triumph with three captured battle flags displayed. Two of his staff rode out of the line to repeat the assurance. What arrived was one man.

By the next day the guard was twenty-five men under Lieutenant David Collins, Company L, Michigan Cavalry. On 5 March a captain of Sheridan’s staff came to state the General’s determination to preserve the University unharmed. Meanwhile the uniform works, Marchant’s factory, the warehouses and the bridges burned.

Of the risk. The Virginia Military Institute had been burned the previous June. Everyone knew it.

Of the tradition. The family holds that guards were posted with orders to fire on any man who tried to burn the place. The archive records the guard, and the courtesy. It does not record the order.

The University of Virginia stands. He was one of the four men who walked out to ask that it be spared.

Division I · The Book

Printed for the author

Institutes of Common and Statute Law · four volumes · Richmond

He set out to state the whole of the common law of Virginia with more precision than anyone had before, and then he paid the printer himself.

There was no publisher standing behind it and no advance. He wrote the treatise, financed the printing, and arranged the distribution through three booksellers. The work went through four editions in his lifetime and shaped the teaching of law across the South for a generation.

Division II · 27 October 1895

The fire

The Rotunda, University of Virginia

Three months after John B. Minor’s death, a fire broke out in the Rotunda Annex, where the law classes met, and spread to the Rotunda.

It destroyed the Annex and the Rotunda, and most of the University’s library with them.

The law books were kept apart from the rest. Raleigh C. Minor saved nearly all of them. The rebuilt Rotunda gave them room in its basement, and they stayed there until the Law School moved to Minor Hall, named for his father, in 1911.

Division II · 1908 – 1910

Built on the Institutes

The Law of Real Property

His own two-volume treatise on real property appeared in 1908, built, as its title records, on his father’s Institutes. Two years later another scholar rebuilt it again on his.

Each generation took the one before as its foundation and built a new story on it.

Division II · 1918

A league before the League

A Republic of Nations · Oxford University Press

A study of the organization of a federal league of nations, published before there was a league, by a professor in Charlottesville.

The First World War was still being fought when the book went to press. The Covenant would not be drafted at the Paris Peace Conference until the following year.

He was not commenting on an institution. He was drafting the organizational law for one that did not exist, in the hope that someone would need it. Oxford published it at once in New York, London, Toronto, Melbourne and Bombay.

Division III · The Last Objection

The question of advertising

1908 – 1977

The idea that a lawyer might advertise was said to have troubled him. It was the settled position of the entire American bar for nearly seventy years, and he held it as a man who had led that bar.

1908
The American Bar Association adopts Canon 27 of the Canons of Professional Ethics, declaring the solicitation of business by advertising unprofessional.
1908 – 1977
The prohibition is adopted by the highest courts of the individual states and holds, substantially unbroken, for nearly seven decades.
1962 – 63
C. Venable Minor presides over the Virginia State Bar under that rule.
1977
Bates v. State Bar of Arizona, 433 U.S. 350, extends commercial-speech protection to attorney advertising. The rule he had defended is struck down.
After
The billboard, the daytime commercial, the injury hotline. He lived another eleven years and saw all of it.

His objection was never to competition. It was to the idea that a person in the worst week of their life should be treated as a market: that distress should be prospected for rather than answered.

Whatever one makes of the rule he defended, the instinct behind it survives intact in the division that follows: what happens to a family should not be the occasion of anyone’s commercial good fortune.
