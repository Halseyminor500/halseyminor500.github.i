Minor on the Law

Four generations of one family’s argument with the law.

Division I · The Teacher

John Barbee Minor

2 June 1813 – 29 July 1895

At sixteen he crossed Virginia on horseback as a newspaper agent, then walked to Ohio to enroll at Kenyon College. Dissatisfied, he walked back.

He took his Bachelor of Laws at the University of Virginia in 1834, aged twenty-one. He returned to its faculty in 1845 and taught there for fifty years, until 1895, the year he died.

Among his students was James Clark McReynolds, later Associate Justice of the Supreme Court of the United States.

Division I · The Flag of Truce

3 March 1865

Sheridan and Custer enter Charlottesville

He was too unwell to attend the meeting at which he was appointed. He went out anyway.

Between one and two o’clock he went with Mayor Christopher Fowler, faculty chairman Socrates Maupin, and Rector Thomas Preston to the ground opposite Carr’s Hill, where a flag of truce had already been raised.

Custer passed in triumph with three captured battle flags displayed. Two of his staff rode out of the line to repeat the assurance. What arrived was one man.

By the next day the guard was twenty-five men under Lieutenant David Collins, Company L, Michigan Cavalry. On 5 March a captain of Sheridan’s staff came to state the General’s determination to preserve the University unharmed. Meanwhile the uniform works, Marchant’s factory, the warehouses and the bridges burned.

The University of Virginia stands. He was one of the four men who walked out to ask that it be spared.

Of the riskThe Virginia Military Institute had been burned the previous June. Everyone knew it.

Of the traditionThe family holds that guards were posted with orders to fire on any man who tried to burn the place. The archive records the guard, and the courtesy. It does not record the order.

Division I · The Book

The Institutes

Four volumes · Richmond · Fourth edition 1891

He set out to state the whole of the common law of Virginia with more precision than anyone had before, and then he paid the printer himself.

There was no publisher standing behind it and no advance. He wrote the treatise, financed the printing, and arranged the distribution through three booksellers. The work went through four editions in his lifetime and shaped the teaching of law across the South for a generation.

Division II · The Architect

Raleigh Colston Minor

1869 – 1923 · Professor of Constitutional and International Law

The second division does not continue the first. It leaves the jurisdiction entirely.

His father had spent fifty years stating the common law of one state with unmatched exactness. The son took the same instrument and pointed it at a body of law with no sovereign, no court, and no existence. For a year, 1907–08, he was acting dean of the Law School.

His own two-volume treatise on real property had appeared in 1908, built, as its title records, on his father’s Institutes. Two years later another scholar rebuilt it again on his. Each generation took the one before as its foundation and built a new story on it.

Of the sonHe did not restate his father. He took the same instrument and pointed it somewhere his father would not have.

Of the marriageHe married Natalie Embra Venable, daughter of Colonel Charles S. Venable of the mathematics faculty. The middle name of the next division descends from her.

Division II · The Book

A Republic of Nations

Oxford University Press · 1918

A study of the organization of a federal league of nations, published before there was a league, by a professor in Charlottesville.

The First World War was still being fought when the book went to press. The Covenant would not be drafted at the Paris Peace Conference until the following year.

He was not commenting on an institution. He was drafting the organizational law for one that did not exist, in the hope that someone would need it. Oxford published it at once in New York, London, Toronto, Melbourne and Bombay.

Division I
Stated the law that existed, for the jurisdiction he lived in.
Division II
Wrote the law for a jurisdiction that did not yet exist, and might never.
In common
Neither man waited for permission, and neither wrote for a market that had asked.

Division III · The Practitioner

Charles Venable Minor

1900 – 1988 · Charlottesville, Virginia · Known to his family as Hippo

The first two divisions taught and wrote. The third practiced, and practice does not leave a title page.

Son of Raleigh Colston Minor and Natalie Embra Venable; grandson of John Barbee Minor. Bachelor of Science, University of Virginia, 1922. Law School, class of 1925. To his family, and to his grandson, he was Hippo.

Of the recordThe practitioner leaves the least paper. The work goes into other people’s matters and stays there.

Of the nameThe family knows him as Hippo. The archive does not.

1962 – 63
President of the Virginia State Bar. His name is on the Bar’s own roll of past presidents, between E. Griffith Dodson, Jr. and William E. White.
The firm
Name partner in Perkins, Battle & Minor of Charlottesville, and afterwards in Battle, Neal, Harris, Minor & Williams.
The Governor
His partner was John Stewart Battle (1890–1972), Governor of Virginia 1950–54, who returned to the Charlottesville practice when his term ended.
1966
The Charlottesville firm merges with Richmond’s McGuire, Woods, King, Davis & Patterson to form McGuire, Woods & Battle, today McGuireWoods.

The firm’s line runs back to a Charlottesville practice opened in 1834 by Egbert R. Watson, once private secretary to President Monroe: the same year John B. Minor took his degree a few hundred yards away.

Division III · The Ode

The hippo and the bird

When Halsey Minor started Minor Ventures, he made its logo a hippo with a small bird on its back. Its companies included GrandCentral, now Google Voice, and OpenDNS.

Division III · The Last Objection

The Question of Advertising

The idea that a lawyer might advertise was said to have troubled him. It was the settled position of the entire American bar for nearly seventy years, and he held it as a man who had led that bar.

1908
The American Bar Association adopts Canon 27 of the Canons of Professional Ethics, declaring the solicitation of business by advertising unprofessional.
1908 – 1977
The prohibition is adopted by the highest courts of the individual states and holds, substantially unbroken, for nearly seven decades.
1962 – 63
C. Venable Minor presides over the Virginia State Bar under that rule.
1977
Bates v. State Bar of Arizona, 433 U.S. 350, extends commercial-speech protection to attorney advertising. The rule he had defended is struck down.
After
The billboard, the daytime commercial, the injury hotline. He lived another eleven years and saw all of it.

His objection was never to competition. It was to the idea that a person in the worst week of their life should be treated as a market: that distress should be prospected for rather than answered.

Whatever one makes of the rule he defended, the instinct behind it survives intact in the division that follows: what happens to a family should not be the occasion of anyone’s commercial good fortune.

Division IV · The Reformation

Halsey Minor

1964 –

He did not go to law school. That is the departure. Everything after it is the return.

The first three divisions accepted the instrument and improved it: stated it more precisely, extended it to new jurisdictions, practiced it honorably, and led the bar that governed it. This one does not accept the instrument.

The common law is a nine-hundred-year-old technology for producing equal justice. The ideal has never been the defect. The delivery mechanism is the defect: an adversarial procedure billed by the hour, which pays its practitioners for duration and charges its users for a resolution most of them cannot afford to reach.

Not changing
The ideal. Equal justice, and the conviction that it is owed rather than sold.
Changing
The venue and the incentive.
The claim
Not a reinvention of the law. A reformation of how ordinary people are required to meet it.

Rethinking publishing and rethinking enterprise software were the same act performed twice: find an industry whose structure, not its people, is producing the bad outcome, and replace the structure. Law is the third instance.

What he is building →

Colophon

The obligation was never the method.

Division I
Stated the law with more precision than anyone before him, walked out under a flag of truce to keep the University standing, and paid for the printing himself.
Division II
Wrote the constitutional law of a federation of nations a year before the federation was drafted into being.
Division III
Practiced, led the bar of Virginia, and objected to the last to anything that would trade the profession’s credibility for its custom.
Division IV
Leaves the courthouse, keeps the ideal, and rebuilds the delivery.

Four generations, one obligation, and no shared method. The first wrote a treatise nobody had asked for. The second wrote the law of a country that did not exist. The third spent forty years inside a system he believed in and defended its dignity when it became unfashionable to. The fourth is dismantling the part of it that stopped serving the people it was built for.

Not going to law school was the departure. This is the return, by the only route now available, and none of the three earlier divisions would have recognized the tools.

They would have recognized the reason.
